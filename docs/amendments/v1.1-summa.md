# Amendment v1.1: SUMMA, one budget across every rail

*Summa, non frustum*: judge the sum, not the slice, and now across chains too.

**Status: proposed, implemented, unaudited.** `src/JudgeSumma.sol` implements S.3–S.7. `test/Summa.t.sol` covers conformance S.12 items 1–6 (15 tests, including a rounding fuzz), and the full suite (242 tests) passes. Item 8 is **done**: a live rehearsal on Coston2 plus XRPL testnet on 2026-09-25 convicted umbrella #17 for $11.22 against $10 across two chains (docs/DEPLOYMENTS.md). `SummaMeter` (S.8) is implemented in `src/SummaMeter.sol` and has 5 tests. It is deployed on Coston2 at `0x6Bc63F3a…4E0f`, where its first live reads valued 2 XRP at $3.072024 and 1 mUSDT0 at $0.999669. `MandateFacilitator` (x402 on Flare: brake, settlement and receipt in one transaction) is also implemented and live (`0xBC545E26…307B`, 5 tests). Item 7 is **done**: `test/invariant/SummaInvariants.t.sol` runs 256 × 80 random filings, with duplicates, inflows, other senders' logs and committed crossings. The docket always equals the sum of distinct deeds and never decreases, the bond's books close, and no verdict exceeds the proportional penalty. `SummaLens` (S.7) is implemented as well: the FLR bond read in dollars after a 30 % haircut, with `covers(k)` and `topUpFor(k)`, fuzzed. It is live at `0xa0A5159b…268B`. All items of S.12 are met. The amendment is not frozen, and it waits for the external audit. It is not frozen. ## S.0 Why this amendment exists

DELICTI's thesis is that a mandate is a **budget**, and that the breach is the **sum**. v1.0 enforces that sum inside one unit on one chain, and it says so:

- §3: *"Non-goal: budgets across several assets under one mandate. One mandate, one unit."*
- §10: *"the children of one parent are not summed: an agent with a budget of 10 can delegate ten children of 10 each."*
- §3, monotonic narrowing: a child must keep its parent's `sourceId` and `assetKey`. A child on another rail is not a smaller share of its parent. It is a different budget.

An agent that holds XRP on the XRP Ledger, USD₮0 and FXRP on Flare, and USDC on Ethereum therefore has three unrelated budgets today. The structuring attack that §6.2 exists to punish is available again at the level above: five slices on five rails, each inside its own limit. A principal who says *"spend at most $500"* cannot write that sentence into DELICTI at all.

SUMMA lets the principal write it.

## S.1 Why only Flare can host it

Summing across rails needs two facts per deed, and a trusted party for neither:

1. **That the deed happened, who did it and how much moved.** This is the FDC (§2), exactly as in §6.10 and §6.11.
2. **What that amount was worth at that moment.** The FTSO Scaling protocol provides this. About 100 data providers commit and reveal every 90-second voting round, and the Merkle root of each round's anchor feeds is written to `Relay`. `FtsoV2.verifyFeedData` checks a value against that root on-chain.

Both are enshrined in the chain DELICTI runs on, and both are secured by the same data providers. Anywhere else, the second fact would be an oracle someone chose, and the sum would inherit that choice.

**Measured on 2026-09-24** (`sdk/scripts/ftso-history.mjs`; results in `docs/research/ftso-history-2026-09-24.md`):

| round age | Coston2 `verifyFeedData` | Flare mainnet `verifyFeedData` |
|---|---|---|
| ~1 h, 1 d, 14 d, 30 d | `true` (FLR/USD, XRP/USD, USDT/USD) | `true` |
| 104 d, 200 d, **365 d** | `true` | `true` |
| value + 1 (tamper) | reverts | reverts |

The developer hub says the DA layer keeps "2 weeks of historical data". It served proofs a year old, and `Relay` still held their roots. The price leg of a case has **no practical horizon**. The deed leg on XRPL has one (~14 days, §10), and the docket (S.6) already handles it. Block-latency reads (`getFeedById`) cost **0 wei** on both networks today (`FeeCalculator.calculateFeeByIds`).

Anchor feeds that exist on both networks and are used here: `FLR/USD`, `XRP/USD`, `ETH/USD`, `BTC/USD`, `USDT/USD`, `USDC/USD`, `SGB/USD`. The FTSO risk tiers published in the developer hub are 0 for USDT and USDC, 1 for FLR, XRP, ETH and BTC, and 2 for SGB. **There is no `RLUSD/USD` feed.**

## S.2 Terms

- **Umbrella.** A v1.0 mandate whose unit is the US dollar. Its budget is the sum SUMMA judges.
- **Member.** A v1.0 mandate on a real rail, linked to an umbrella. Its deeds count toward the umbrella's sum. It keeps its own budget, its own Vault and its own bond, and all of those keep working exactly as before.
- **Link.** The on-chain statement that a member's deeds, from a given moment on, count toward an umbrella.
- **Micro-dollar (µUSD).** The umbrella's unit: 1 USD = 10⁶ µUSD.
- **Price map.** The immutable table that tells `JudgeSumma` which anchor feed values which asset, and how many decimals that asset has.

## S.3 The umbrella mandate

An umbrella is an ordinary registry mandate (§3), committed with:

| field | value |
|---|---|
| `sourceId` | `bytes32("SUMMA")`. It is nonzero, as `commit` requires, and no FDC source carries it, so no v1.0 judge can ever match a proof to an umbrella. |
| `assetKey` | `bytes32("USD/1e6")` |
| `budget` | in µUSD |
| `agent` | the party that answers for the sum: the agent itself when one agent spans several rails, or the operator of a fleet |
| `bond` | a `VaultSumma` (S.7) |

Everything in §3 holds unchanged: acknowledgement by the agent, revocation, transitive liveness, the window. An umbrella's registry children are narrower umbrellas (same marker, a budget ≤ the parent's). As in v1.0, those children are not summed with each other. A principal who wants that sum links rail mandates to one umbrella instead.

## S.4 Linking: who may put what under an umbrella

*Implementation note:* linking lives in `JudgeSumma.link`. It is not a separate `SummaLink` contract, because the judge must read the membership it checks, and a second contract would only add an address to trust.

`link(umbrellaId, memberId)` succeeds only if:

1. The caller is the umbrella's **agent**. The agent is the party whose bond answers for the sum, so linking is its decision.
2. `member.principal == umbrella.principal`. One principal speaks for both budgets.
3. The member is **acknowledged** by its own agent.
4. The member is **watchable without receipts**, which means one of:
   - a gross-XRP-outflow mandate (`assetKey = "XRP/outflow"`) whose XRPL key proved exclusivity (§6.10, `AgentRefs.proveExclusive`);
   - an ERC-20 mandate on an EVM source whose agent declared exclusivity (§6.11).

   Receipted members (§6.2, §6.3, §6.8) are out of scope. Their deed set is whatever the agent wrote down, and a sum over a self-chosen set is a sum the agent chooses.
5. The member's `(sourceId, assetKey)` is in the **price map** (S.5).
6. The umbrella has fewer than `MAX_MEMBERS` (16) members.

Two more rules:

- **A link is sticky.** An unlink would be a way to hide the deeds of the rail about to overrun.
- **A link counts forward only.** A deed counts toward the umbrella if its timestamp `t` satisfies:

  ```
  max(umbrella.validFrom, member.validFrom, linkedAt)  ≤  t  ≤  min(umbrella.validUntil, member.validUntil)
  ```

  Without `linkedAt`, linking a member with a large past outflow would convict the umbrella the moment it was linked. That would be the collusion of §8.3 with fewer steps.

## S.5 Valuation

**The price map is code.** `JudgeSumma` fixes it at construction:

```
(sourceId, assetKey) → (feedId, assetDecimals)
```

It never reads it from calldata, from the envelope or from the link. The party being judged must not be able to choose which price values its spending: it would choose the lowest. A new asset means a new `JudgeSumma` and a new `VaultSumma`, the same way a new kind means a new Vault (§8.2).

| rail asset | feed | decimals |
|---|---|---|
| XRP on XRPL (`XRP/outflow`) | `XRP/USD` | 6 |
| USD₮0 on Flare | `USDT/USD` | 6 |
| USDC.e on Flare, USDC on Ethereum | `USDC/USD` | 6 |
| FXRP on Flare | `XRP/USD` | 6 |
| WFLR on Flare | `FLR/USD` | 18 |
| WETH on Ethereum | `ETH/USD` | 18 |

(The testnet deployment maps the testnet sources and the MockUSDT0 used by v0.13.)

**The round is a function of the deed.** For a deed at timestamp `t` (the FDC's `timestamp` or `blockTimestamp`):

```
round(t) = floor((t − firstVotingRoundStartTs) / votingEpochDurationSeconds)
```

Both parameters are read live from `ProtocolsV2`, the same clock §6.7 already trusts, with the same fail-closed rule when that clock is not linear. The price proof must be for exactly `round(t)`. Nobody chooses a round.

**The arithmetic rounds in the agent's favour.** For each deed:

```
value(µUSD) = floor( amount × feedValue × 10^6 / 10^(assetDecimals + feedDecimals) )
```

The division is done last, with overflow-checked 512-bit multiplication. A negative `feedDecimals` is handled symmetrically. Rounding down on every deed means rounding can never convict. The most it can do is let an agent pass by less than one µUSD per deed.

**Stablecoins are priced, not assumed.** USD₮0 at `USDT/USD = 0.99966` counts as 0.99966 USD. The budget stays honest through a depeg, in both directions.

**Proofs are cached.** A verified `(feedId, round) → value` is stored the first time it is proven, so one price proof serves every deed in that round, on any umbrella.

## S.6 The docket and the crossing: kind 9 (`JudgeSumma.fileSumma`)

```
fileSumma(umbrellaId, memberId, deedProofs[], priceProofs[], salt)
```

- **One filing, one member, one attestation type.** The EVM member files `EVMTransaction` proofs, and the XRPL member files `BalanceDecreasingTransaction` proofs. This keeps the checks exactly those of §6.11 and §6.10 respectively. It also lets the Vault's reimbursement (`fdcCost(type, source, n)`) price the filing correctly.
- **Each deed is checked by its member's rules.**
  - ERC-20: live `Transfer(agent → anyone, v)` logs of the member's token, keyed per `(tx, logIndex)`, with 64 confirmations on Ethereum.
  - XRP: positive `spentAmount`, where the source address indicator and the hash are both the member's `agentRef`.
  - Deeds that move nothing count nothing (§8.4).
- **Each deed is valued** with the price proof for `round(t)` (S.5).
- **Identity is per umbrella, not per member.** The docket keys deeds by `(sourceId, txId, logIndex)` for ERC-20 and by `(sourceId, txId)` for XRP. Two members that both claim the same address therefore cannot make one transfer count twice. Already-filed deeds are skipped, not refused, as in §6.10.
- **Below the budget, a filing records.** No commitment is needed and nothing is paid, except stipends (§8.4), which work unchanged through `Vault.requestAttestation` and `stipend`.
- **The filing that takes the docket past the budget is the conviction:**
  - commitment kind **9**, digest over the deed ids it supplies (ascending);
  - the lowest voting round is taken over the FDC proofs that added something;
  - severity = `docket − budget` in µUSD, nested (its own bucket, high-water mark);
  - `Vault.wouldTake` is asked first, exactly as in v0.15.

## S.7 `VaultSumma`: no new core code

`Vault.verdict(kind, mandateId, budget, severity, …)` never interprets the unit. `_penalty` takes `base × severity / budget`, a ratio of two numbers in the same unit, applied to a bond in the chain's native asset. A USD budget and a USD severity therefore slash the same **fraction** of an FLR bond that an XRP budget and an XRP severity would. `Kinds.bucket(9)` is 9 and `Kinds.additive(9)` is false, so kind 9 gets its own nested bucket without touching `Kinds`.

`VaultSumma` is therefore **the v0.15 `Vault` bytecode**, deployed with `judges = [JudgeSumma]`. Umbrellas name it in `Terms.bond`. Nothing in the audited core changes. The only new code with power over money is `JudgeSumma`, and it also holds membership (`link`).

### The bond is posted in FLR, and read in dollars

The bond stays in the chain's native asset, **FLR**. This is deliberate. The chain that makes the sum provable is also the collateral the sum is written against. A bond in FLR has one weakness, and the spike shows its size: FLR/USD was 0.0269 a year ago (round 1114664) and 0.0072 today, about −73 %. A bond sized at `k = 2` in dollars a year ago would cover about half the budget now.

The protocol does not pretend otherwise. It does two things, both outside the core:

1. **`SummaLens.coverage(umbrellaId)` reads the bond in dollars, live.**

   ```
   coverage = bondOf × FLR/USD(block-latency) × (1 − haircut(riskTier(FLR)))
   ```

   The haircut table is immutable per lens version:

   | FTSO risk tier | haircut |
   |---|---|
   | 0 (USDT, USDC) | 2 % |
   | 1 (FLR, XRP, ETH, BTC) | 30 % |
   | 2 | 50 % |

2. **The requirement belongs to whoever relies on the bond.** A facilitator, a guard, a credential issuer or a venue reads `coverage ≥ k × budget` at the moment it extends trust, and refuses otherwise. When FLR falls, the agent tops up with `post`, or it loses service. This is the market's version of FAssets' minimal collateral ratio, and it needs no liquidation, because the bond backs no minted asset.

Later, and **not in this amendment**, a Vault that accepts ERC-20 collateral (USD₮0; and sFLR, which keeps earning staking yield while it guards, valued live by the Sceptre `sFLR/USD` custom feed) removes the volatility altogether. That is new code with power over money, so it waits for its own audit.

## S.8 `SummaMeter`: the brake, across rails

`SpendMeter` (§7.1) refuses the fifth slice on one rail. `SummaMeter` refuses it across all of them:

- `wouldExceed(umbrellaId, feedId, amount, decimals)` values the amount with the **block-latency** feed (`FtsoV2.getFeedById`, free today) and compares it with the umbrella's tally.
- `note(...)` appends a checkpoint `(timestamp, µUSD total)`, and `spentAt(umbrellaId, ts)` reads the past.
- The principal declares which effectors may write, as in §7.1.

The meter is **one witness**. It never convicts on its own. A kind-5-style "under-reported sum" is deliberately **not** part of this amendment (see S.10). The guard (Lancea's co-signer on XRPL) and the x402 facilitator on Flare are the intended writers. They read the umbrella's tally before they sign or settle, so the fifth slice stops at the moment it would cross, whichever rail it is on.

## S.9 Trust model: what is added to §2

**One enshrined protocol: FTSO Scaling.** It is run by the same data providers, under the same signing policy, with its roots in the same `Relay` as the FDC. SUMMA adds no signer, no committee, no bridge and no admin key. The price map and the haircut table are immutable per deployment.

## S.10 What SUMMA does not claim

- **RLUSD and other XRPL issued currencies.** There is no FTSO feed and no FDC type that sees them (§10). A class-R evidence route (public RPC through Web2Json) and a custom `RLUSD/USD` feed (FIP.13) are both conceivable. Neither is part of this amendment.
- **Base and other chains the FDC does not index.** Same answer.
- **Native FLR moved by internal calls.** `EVMTransaction` shows only top-level value. An agent that spends through SUMMA should hold **WFLR**, an ERC-20, which §6.11 sees completely.
- **The price within a round.** Valuation is at 90-second granularity. An agent can time a deed inside a round. It cannot choose the round.
- **Turnout.** An anchor value is what the providers who turned out agreed on. `turnoutBIPS` is published with every proof, and SUMMA does not second-guess it.
- **XRPL deeds older than the verifier remembers (~14 days)** are lost to the case unless filed in time. The price leg does not add a horizon (S.1).
- **Receipted members.** They are out of scope (S.4 rule 4).
- **An under-reported sum.** A meter that lies about the umbrella tally is not convicted by SUMMA. It is caught per rail by §6.5 where a rail meter exists, and otherwise it is simply outvoted by the docket, which does not depend on the meter.
- **The umbrella's children are not summed** (§10 still holds for them). Link rails to one umbrella instead.
- **The bond's dollar value.** It floats with FLR/USD. Coverage is a live reading (S.7), not a promise.
- **Assets outside the price map** need a new `JudgeSumma`. That is the price of the map being code.

## S.11 The kinds freeze

Kind 9 is the last kind added under v1. Every future evidence source (class R, class B+ in a TEE, a new FDC type) enters as a new *judge* for an existing kind's quantity, or it waits for v2. Eight kinds, two judges and three dockets were already the protocol's largest risk surface. SUMMA is admitted because it completes the thesis. It does not start a new one.

## S.12 Conformance (tests that must exist before this is frozen)

1. **Sum.** Umbrella docket = Σ `value(deed)` over every distinct filed deed, and no deed is counted twice, across members, filings and proof splits.
2. **Rounding.** For every deed, `value ≤ exact` and `exact − value < 1 µUSD`. A fuzz over prices, decimals (including negative) and amounts.
3. **Round binding.** A price proof for any round ≠ `round(t)` is refused, and so is a tampered value (`verifyFeedData` reverts).
4. **Link consent.** Only the umbrella's agent can link; a principal mismatch, an unacknowledged member, a receipted member and an unmapped asset are all refused. Links cannot be removed. Deeds before `linkedAt` count nothing.
5. **Unit-free slash.** For the same overrun fraction, `VaultSumma` takes the same fraction of the bond as the v0.15 Vault does for a single-rail mandate.
6. **Isolation.** No v0.15 judge can convict an umbrella (the marker `sourceId`), and `JudgeSumma` cannot convict a rail mandate.
7. **Books.** The v0.15 invariant campaign runs unchanged against `VaultSumma`, with `JudgeSumma` added to the handler.
8. **Live rehearsal on Coston2.** An umbrella of $N over an XRPL member (XRP outflow) and a Flare member (MockUSDT0 outflow). Three slices on each rail, each within its rail budget, and the sum crosses the umbrella. Conviction by kind 9, with the prices shown in the verdict's deeds.
